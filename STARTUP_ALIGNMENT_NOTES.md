# Microsoft for Startups alignment notes

These notes are for ENASH internally; they are not rendered on the public website.

Microsoft's current public eligibility guidance says an eligible company must develop a software-based product or service owned by the company, be privately held and for-profit, and must not be a consultancy or agency. The application guidance also describes the program as designed for early-stage B2B startups building software, AI or technology solutions and working toward or already at MVP.

Official references:

- https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/startups/microsoft-for-startups/overview
- https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/startups/microsoft-for-startups/application

## How this website is structured

- ENASH is presented as a technology product company.
- The homepage leads with ENASH-owned products rather than client work.
- Yenza AI, TaxiFind, KitchCore and What's There? each have dedicated product pages.
- Live deployments are linked where they exist.
- Company registration information is visible on the Company page.
- Public navigation has no consultancy, agency, quotation, request-a-project or bespoke-development section.
- Contact topics are product feedback, partnership/integration, investment, media and company enquiries.
- The ENASH Assistant is hard-coded to describe the actual product portfolio and not invent traction or offer bespoke client development.

## Important

This structure should reflect the real current business model. Do not add false customer counts, revenue, funding, partnerships, product capabilities or ownership claims for the purpose of verification. Microsoft makes the final eligibility decision, so no website wording can guarantee approval.
